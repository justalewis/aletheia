Lys.

lys
Danish and Norwegian for light.
alētheia
Greek for truth. Taken literally, unconcealment: whatever is no longer hidden.
the idea
Put the readings and their licenses in plain light, and keep everything else quiet.

Chalk-white pages, one cobalt blue, large grotesk type, and plain indexes where the site now uses cards.

Drawn from Hello Monday (Copenhagen, Aarhus) and MBRS (Helsinki, Berlin) · 18 Oct 2026 · A proposal only: nothing in the app has changed

Direction

Two studios, one library

Both reference sites are portfolios, and Aletheia is a working tool with about 2,200 readings in it. What carries over is their restraint: a strict grid, very few colors, type doing the work that boxes and shadows usually do, and motion reserved for the moment it explains something.

Copenhagen · Aarhus

Hello Monday

The work index: big titles in a single column, a one-line text filter above them (Products, Experiences, Branding), plenty of air between rows, and hover effects only on the row under the pointer.

In Aletheia: the catalog, focus pages, and outcome pages become indexes of readings in place of card grids. The filter bar is words, not dropdowns.

Helsinki · Berlin

MBRS

Two colors, black and one blue (#1D47BF). Accordions that open in place so you never lose your spot. Wide margins and a grid you can feel even when nothing marks it.

In Aletheia: course units in the builder and the reader's table of contents open in place. Cobalt marks only what you can act on and where you are.

Left behind

Preloaders, custom cursors, WebGL scenes, and scroll-jacked parallax. Instructors open Aletheia on lab computers, older laptops, and classroom projectors, and some use a keyboard or screen reader. Every page loads straight to its content and works with motion turned off.

Four rulesEvery later decision should trace back to one of these.

  • Paper and sheet

    The site sits on chalk. White sheets appear only where something is a document: a reading, an export, the reader itself.

  • One blue

    Cobalt marks the next action and your current place. Links in running text stay ink with an underline and turn cobalt on hover.

  • Type over boxes

    Size, weight, and space carry the hierarchy. No shadows and almost no borders; hairlines separate rows in an index and little else.

  • Licenses in plain sight

    Every reading shows its reuse glyph and license string in the same spot on every page, from catalog row to printed reader.

Tokens

Chalk, ink, cobalt

Eight colors, three typefaces, one spacing scale. The neutrals lean slightly blue so they sit with the accent instead of against it. The cream ground and Charter serif the app uses now go; a serif stays only inside the reader.

Discourse Community Study

Students pick a discourse community they belong to or want to join, interview a member, and analyze the community's goals, vocabulary, and genres. This is the first unit with primary research, so it also introduces interviewing and research ethics.

  • Plan and conduct an interview with a community member.

Rollout

Getting it into the app

The current stylesheet already runs on tokens, so most of Lys is a change of values. Five steps, each its own pull request, in this order.

Steps

  1. Tokens and fonts

    Swap the values in the :root and dark blocks of frontend/src/styles.css, and add the three families through @fontsource packages so no request goes to Google. This step alone changes most of the look.

  2. Header, navigation, and type scale

    Wordmark left, text nav right, a cobalt square on the current page. Replace the heading sizes with the scale above; drop the card shadows.

  3. Indexes and reuse glyphs

    Catalog, focus, arc, and outcome pages move from card grids to index rows. TierBadge becomes the glyph plus the license string.

  4. Builder ruler and unit accordions

    TermBar becomes the ruler; UnitCard becomes an accordion row. The six pastel segment colors go: frost for units, cobalt for the open one.

  5. Reader and print

    reader.css and reader_print.html take Literata, the white sheet, and the mono credit lines. Glyphs print correctly in black and white.

Timing. Other sessions are editing the app right now. Land step 1 after their branches merge, since it rewrites the top of styles.css that every page depends on.
